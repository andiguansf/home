When Breath Becomes Air

A personal reflection

I first read When Breath Becomes Air in 2020/2021, during the throes of the Covid-19 pandemic. I've always been drawn to books reflecting on mortality - initially because I felt like I couldn't waste my life but now I read them in search for meaning. What gave others' meaning during their lives, what gave them purpose, how did they define a life well lived? I sought answers in memoirs because I couldn't find answers in my real life. Earlier this year, I was 28 and honestly quite lost. I had everything on paper - a good partner, a well-paid career, and was living in one of the best places on earth. I had climbed the mountain, achieved my goals, and had nothing to prove anymore. I couldn't understand why I didn't feel happy. Then in July, I was diagnosed with cancer. It was probably the best thing to happen to me.

When the future changes shape

Perhaps the question is not simply how much time we have, but what we choose to give our time to.

About the book: When Breath Becomes Air is a memoir by Paul Kalanithi, a neurosurgeon who reflects on life, illness, and mortality after being diagnosed with terminal cancer. This essay is a personal reflection on the book’s themes, not an excerpt from the original work.
